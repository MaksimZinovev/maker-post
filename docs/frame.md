# Maker Post — Frame

## Source

### Andrei — User Interview

> "I work on a pack of projects. All of them are happening in parallel... In the work there are, I don't know, five, seven projects. From the stage where I draw a sketch to the finished product... it would be good to take [photos] and group them: product 1, 2, 3." — [Source 3]

> "Because when you make a video, you don't have to think. You spend—basically, you have to do a lot of micro-movements. You take a photo, you need to set: 'Okay, this is a photo for this project, I'll put it in this folder.' Then you have to think: 'Okay, I've accumulated some photos, can I make a post out of them?'... sitting and spending an hour to make a post manually is not a problem for me. But the fact that you constantly need to remember things, organize something..." — [Source 7]

> "For me, for example, it's absolutely not important that he [the AI] writes the post text himself. I can write that, but if he gathers all the information in one place and gives it to me, saying, 'this can be posted about this'..." — [Source 4]

> "It would be like this: he would say, 'Well, you can make a post about this,' and you go in and you already have all your media folded in one folder. You don't have to climb and search for when you photographed it." — [Source 8]

> "I have a VN editor in my phone... I open it, select photos and videos I want to edit, and assemble them... this is actually the least burdensome for me. It's even a pleasant activity, I would say." — [Source 9]

> "I buy some new plastic, I need to test it, set it up, configure it. So I need to store it somewhere. I mean, I do a bunch of experiments. I need to record their results, because after a while [I'll forget]." — [Source 15]

> "I want to write down what I've learned... so I have a reference of what I can do, so I can look in there." — [Source 16]

> "He [the AI] can look at what other popular videos on this topic are. And when it suggests ideas for my next video, it can take that into account." — [Source 21]

> "The most popular posts—it's people printing colored flower pots, children's toys, basically junk that my target audience won't be interested in, because my target audience is people or companies who want to solve engineering problems." — [Source 23]

> "That's already too much. That's already too much... I can just take a photo. Everything else should happen by itself." — [Source 27]

> "Probably, this is where most of the time goes—on organization... Brain fuel is spent on this moment." — [Source 34]

> "I wake up in the morning... open a page and he says: 'These are the ideas we've accumulated. These are the groups you could post.' Then you posted it, and it moved from 'to be posted' to 'posted.'" — [Source 35]

> "If he created an album for me for each project... I could say 'select all' and shove it into my [editor]. Yes, that's ideal." — [Source 39]

> "There should be a link between the idea and the album... if your app has this [share] button, then it's basically enough. You can immediately do it in Instagram or in your video editor." — [Source 41]

> "It would also be nice if this assistant suggested which photos are missing. For example, if it sees that a post can already be made for some project, it might ask to take a few more photos... 'there is a good post idea, but these photos are missing. Please make a couple.'" — [Source 47]

## Pre-work: Options Landscape

The original shaping (docs/shaping.md v1) explored a different problem: AI caption generation with minimal friction. The interview reframes this. Two options surfaced:

| Option | What it does | Who benefits | Signal strength |
|--------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| **A. AI caption generator** | Photo → AI writes caption → approve → post to Instagram | Solo makers who want content but can't write | Moderate — the original idea; but Andrei explicitly de-prioritized it [Source 4, 18, 19] |
| **B. AI organizational assistant** | Auto-classify photos by project → surface post ideas → track status → reference library | Solo makers juggling multiple projects who lose mental fuel to organization | Strong — Andrei repeatedly and emphatically described this as the core pain [Source 7, 34, 27] |

**Why B now:** The interview is unambiguous. Andrei's pain is not "I can't write captions" — it's "I lose brain fuel organizing photos across 5–7 parallel projects." He explicitly says AI caption writing is "absolutely not important" [Source 4] and that editing is "a pleasant activity" [Source 9]. The organizational burden is what drains him. Option A is not wrong — it's just not the urgent problem. AI caption generation stays as an optional, off-by-default feature.

## Problem

- Existing tools are "heavyweight" — complex setups, steep learning curves that don't fit a maker's workflow [Source 2]
- The "micro-movements" of organization — sorting photos into folders, remembering which project each belongs to — drain mental fuel [Source 7, 34]
- Andrei manages 5–7 projects simultaneously, from sketch to finished product, making manual tracking untenable [Source 3]
- No automatic grouping exists: Andrei has to manually search for "when he photographed it" [Source 8]
- There's no clear view of what's ready to post vs. what's already been posted [Source 35]
- Experiment results (filament configs, print settings) are not recorded and are forgotten over time [Source 15]
- Content suggestions ignore his actual audience (engineers/companies, not hobbyists printing "junk") [Source 23]

## Outcome

- Photos auto-classified by project with zero manual tagging — "I can just take a photo, everything else happens by itself" [Source 27]
- Post ideas surfaced when enough content accumulates per project [Source 32, 35]
- Clear workspace: "to be posted" → "posted" lifecycle tracked [Source 35]
- Grouped media accessible so Andrei can "select all" and move to his editor [Source 39]
- Technical experiment results stored as a reference library for future projects [Source 15, 16]
- Andrei focuses on the "pleasant" creative work of editing and storytelling, not the organizational drudgery [Source 9]

---

## Less about

- AI writing captions or generating content on Andrei's behalf (he writes his own [Source 4, 18, 19])
- Replacing the creative editing process (VN editor is "a pleasant activity" [Source 9])
- Complex multi-app setups with steep learning curves [Source 2]
- Generic AI-generated content (slop) [Source 23]
- Instagram auto-posting in MVP (Andrei creates videos himself; posting is a future enhancement)

## More about

- Removing the organizational friction that drains "brain fuel" [Source 34]
- Auto-grouping media by project so Andrei can "select all" and create [Source 39]
- A reference library that compounds knowledge over time [Source 15, 16]
- Surfacing what's ready to post and what's missing [Source 35, 47]
- Audience-aware content strategy (engineers/companies, not hobbyists) [Source 23]
